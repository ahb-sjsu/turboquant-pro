Dear Editor:

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Equal Weight Error, Unequal Effect: Quantization
Sensitivity at the Consuming Operator,'' for publication as a full paper in the IEEE
Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems. I am the sole author.

Weight quantization of language models is often guided by a ranking of a model's components
built from statistics of their weights. The manuscript reads sensitivity at the model's output
instead, one consuming operator at a time, and reports that equal weight errors have unequal
effects. With one quantizer at the same bit width, the four attention projections of four
language models have similar weight errors, yet perturbing the value and output projections
moves the next-token distribution 2.4 to 3.8 times more than perturbing the query and key
projections. A factorial on two models traces a recently reported opposite ranking to the bit
allocation of shipped quantization presets. The ordering also depends on the quantizer: a
second row scale changes it, and GPTQ, which compensates each layer's output error, keeps the
ordering of each row scale on two models. Two further studies, of top-$k$ routers in
mixture-of-experts models and of the decay of state-space models, show the same dependence on
the operator that reads the tensor.

The manuscript does not propose a quantizer. Its contribution is a set of measurements under
declared protocols, with intervals where the design allows them, and with the results that
went against my earlier readings reported as such. A reproduction notebook rebuilds every
reported number from the run records and compares it with the printed value, and a Lean 4 file
checks some of the identities the text relies on.

The submission consists of four files. The title page gives the author, the affiliation, the
address for correspondence and the acknowledgment. The manuscript is anonymous and is 13 pages
in the two-column format. The supplementary material is 5 pages and holds the detail that the
page limit moved out of the manuscript, each piece summarized in the manuscript. The code
archive is anonymized and contains the experiment code, the run records and the notebook.
