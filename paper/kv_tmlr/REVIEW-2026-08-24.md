### B4. Compression-ratio claim is wrong twice

"one extra fp32 scalar per channel … negligible (4-bit compression ratio 7.9×,
**identical to NF4**)":

- Not identical. At group 128 with fp32 metadata: NF4 = 7.53×, asym-NF4 =
  7.11× vs fp32 (3.76× → 3.56× vs fp16) — a **5.6% relative overhead**.
- 7.9× is only reachable against **fp32** at group ≳256; the paper never states
  the baseline, and KV caches are usually fp16, where the ceiling is 4×.

**Fixed** with computed numbers at group 128 and the baseline stated
explicitly; adjust if your group size differs. (`DRAFT.md` says fp16 metadata,
`main.tex` says fp32 — pick one.)

### B5. `DRAFT.md` and `main.tex` disagree on a headline number

## C. Machine-checked results (new)

`paper/kv_tmlr/lean/AsymNF4.lean` — **12 theorems, 0 `sorry`, builds clean**
against Mathlib on the Atlas workstation (`lake build`, 8655 jobs). Verified
that the file contains no `sorry` and that every theorem elaborates.

The paper's mechanism section makes geometric claims that were stated
informally. They are now proved, for *any* 16-level grid (so the results cover
NF4, uniform, and any other codebook — they concern grid **placement**, not
level spacing):

| Theorem | Paper claim it discharges |
|---|---|
| `range_div_absmax_lt_one`, `range_div_absmax_symmetric`, `not_symmetric_of_ratio_lt_one` | range/abs-max ≈ 0.9 is *evidence of one-sidedness*: ratio < 1 entails one-sided data; symmetric-about-zero data has ratio exactly 2 |
| `dead_code`, `dead_code_forall` | "a symmetric codebook wastes half of its codes on the empty side" — any grid point below the data minimum is never the nearest code, hence never emitted |
| `dead_span_fraction`, `dead_span_at_measured_ratio` | the dead fraction is exactly (2−ρ)/2, **= 0.55 at your measured ρ = 0.9** — "about half" is now exact |
| `absmax_sub_ge_halfRange`, `midpoint_attains_halfRange` | the zero-point is *optimal*, not merely helpful: no offset beats the half-range, and the midpoint attains it |
| `dequant_error` | the dequantization identity: reconstruction error = scale × normalized rounding error |
| `error_ratio`, `error_ratio_gt_two` | placement alone costs a factor 2b/(b−a) = **2/ρ = 2.22× at ρ = 0.9**, and always > 2 for one-sided data |

**Scope, stated in the file and in the new §6:** these bound worst-case scalar
reconstruction error. They say nothing about GQA amplification, the perplexity
collapse, or the measured 3–5× (which mixes placement with NF4's Gaussian level
spacing). The gap between the proved 2.22× and the measured 3–5× is now
*explained* in the paper as the level-spacing contribution rather than left
implicit — a strengthening, since it separates what is proved from what is
measured.
