We report a measurement of routed inverted-file search over an index of one trillion 4-bit quantized vectors, built and queried on the National Research Platform's shared Kubernetes cluster. The index is 500 servers of two billion rows each, 24.0 bytes per row, 24 TB in all, on one block volume per server. Against the exact full scan of the same compressed index, routed search returns recall at ten of 0.989 when it probes 32 of 2048 cells and 0.999 when it probes 128, on 100 queries, with 95 percent bootstrap intervals of 0.982 to 0.995 and 0.997 to 1.000. The same protocol gave 0.979 and 0.999 at one hundred million rows, 0.982 and 0.999 at ten billion, and 0.983 and 0.999 at one hundred billion. Recall against the full scan does not degrade across four orders of magnitude of corpus size. Inside the trillion-row index, where subsets of servers differ only in the number of rows, recall moves by less than 0.01 at 128 probes and rises with the number of rows at 16 and 32 probes. Every scale point draws its queries by the same recipe, the first rows of four seeded shards spread across the index, so the comparison across sizes holds the query distribution fixed. That recipe also makes every query a corpus row, with a sixth of the reference neighbours on one server. A second set of 100 queries drawn under seeds no corpus shard uses, so that none is in the index and their neighbours spread over 404 servers, gave 0.969 at 32 probes and 0.999 at 128, with intervals of 0.954 to 0.982 and 0.997 to 1.000. The second result is that recall on the corpus-row queries is a property of the router alone. From the cell of each reference neighbour in its query's probe order we predicted recall at nine probe widths without a scan, the prediction matched the two widths already measured to the last digit, and a sweep at three further widths, run after the prediction was recorded, returned 0.952, 0.997 and 1.000, each equal to its predicted value. The measure is self-consistency of the compressed index on a synthetic corpus of intrinsic dimension 16, and the paper gives the float ground truth and real-embedding figures that bound it. Reranking each shortlist against float rows regenerated from the seed shows that the compressed ranking keeps at most 0.621 of the true neighbours on this corpus, and after that rerank the routed top ten agrees with the reranked reference at 0.982 for 32 probes and 1.000 from 128 on. The build and the job design that made the measurement possible on a shared cluster, almost entirely in its smallest job class, are described in a companion paper. Every log, state file, partial result and score is in the repository, and the identity and the bound the analysis rests on are checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
