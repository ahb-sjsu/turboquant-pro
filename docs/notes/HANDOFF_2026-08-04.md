## 1b. Why it now converges despite the enforcer

Measured 08:50: builds that reach real work survive (build-1, build-2 and
build-7 ran 15-17 minutes and wrote 82-83 shards each, sitting near 4074Mi of
their 4Gi request, so their utilization is fine). Builds still in the
low-memory `resume-skip` phase get killed. Shards already written persist on
the volume and `resume-skip` picks them up, so **a kill costs only the shard in
flight.**

The original `MAXTRIES=6` treated those kills as failures and aborted the whole
run after six. That is backwards. With `TQP_MAXTRIES=1000` the driver simply
re-issues, each attempt resumes further along, and the run grinds to completion
through the enforcer rather than fighting it. Progress before the relaunch:
82, 83, 39, 31, 14, 14, 13 shards across the wave.

This does not remove the value of fixing the startup trough (section 2) — that
would make each attempt survive instead of being re-issued — but it is no
longer a blocker.

## 2. The enforcer, and why it looked like everything else

NRP deletes the **Job object** of any pod whose usage sits outside its request.
Bands, user-confirmed: CPU 20-200%, memory 20-150%. A pod is exempt entirely at
cpu <= 1 AND mem <= 2 GiB.

**The enforcer averages over roughly five minutes before it acts.** Measured
from six kill rounds in the event log: three fleet rounds of exactly 8 pods at
08:18:51, 08:24:28 and 08:29:03, gaps of 337s and 275s, and a solo build killed
at 6m21s. Pods live ~5 minutes and then die together.

That explains every failure and why nothing I tried worked. A build opens with a
**~4 minute `resume-skip` scan at ~150Mi**, which is 3.7% of a 4Gi request. By
the time the enforcer has enough samples to decide, the average it holds is
dominated by that startup phase. It kills the pod just as real work begins.

This is an age-of-information problem, not a sizing problem. I spent the session
optimising the instantaneous value the enforcer never looks at.

**A separate, transient clamp also existed.** For several hours anything above
2 GiB was deleted within ~60s regardless of usage (seven pods, one variable;
3Gi and 4Gi probes died before they finished allocating). It had lifted by
08:07 — a 3Gi probe then ran for minutes. Do not assume it is gone; re-test
before trusting a large request.

### The fix that follows from the model

The first five minutes must look like steady state. Either:

1. **Allocate the output buffer before the resume-skip loop** so memory starts
   near 700Mi instead of 150Mi. At a 3Gi request that is 23%, above the floor.
   Needs the peak to fit 3Gi, which currently it does not (see below).
2. **Get the whole run into the ignored range** (cpu<=1, mem<=2Gi) by streaming
   shard output to disk instead of materialising the full float32 block. Then no
   averaging applies at all. This is the robust option.

Measured profile after this session's fixes: **trough 1030Mi, peak 2948Mi**
(`cur` max ~3609Mi). At 4Gi that is 25% / 72%, inside the bands in steady state
— but the startup trough is what kills it.

## 5. The corpus caveat that governs how 1T is reported

Measured this session (`FLEET_CORPUS_GEOMETRY.md`): the fleet corpus is
intrinsic dimension **15.4-17.0, flat at every scale**, against 52-61 for real
embeddings. It is a rank-16 Gaussian at dim 32.

1T on this corpus is a **scaling-mechanics result** — the machinery works at
10^12. It must NOT be reported as evidence that 4-bit compression preserves
recall on realistic data.

**There is already a better measurement.** `real_pilot_15M_wiki1024.json`, real
Cohere Embed-V3:

| | synthetic 1B | real 15M |
|---|---|---|
| recall vs ADC fullscan | 0.999 | 0.9898 |
| **true recall, ADC only** | **0.592** | **0.689** |
| true recall, reranked | 0.991 | 0.981 |

The headline 0.999 is recall against ADC full scan, a self-consistency measure.
True recall is 0.6-0.7 and reranking is what recovers it. Scaling the *real*
pilot from 15M to 100M-1B answers the interesting question far better than 1T
on Gaussians, and it needs no generator.

Note for any realistic-geometry plan: dim 1024 means **19.1 GiB per 5M-row
shard** versus 0.6 GiB today. Index size is unchanged (set by bytes-per-vector)
but the build working set is 32x. This session's memory work is a prerequisite
for that, not made moot by it.

## 6. RC-1 generator — reviewed, and not hopeless

See `openvector-bench/results/GEN_ROUND12_STAGE2.md` for the full round-12
record including tonight's addendum.

**Achieved, and real:** round 8 hit its registered target — six gates in
[0.5, 2.0]x AND anatomy in band on three fresh seeds, base-to-base skew
1.38-1.91 against real's 1.50. Round 9 passed B-battery G6 growth exponents at
k=10/30, a campaign first, with G3 at 1.00 in 24/24 cells. Every gate has been
solved individually at some point. The honest gap: round 8 matched at 2x
tolerance; admission needs [0.85, 1.15].

**The failures are one finding, discovered three times:** anything calibrated
under one sampling operator fails under another. Round 9 (fit-at-n does not
survive subsampling), round 11 (planted counts do not), round 12 (a mechanism
verified at n=3000/dim=64 does not survive n=200k/dim=1024). That is publishable
methodology in its own right and arguably worth more than the generator.

**Round 12 is untested, not refuted.** The cascade never instantiated at ladder
scale (1.99 octaves against the >=3 its own claim needs) so the presence gate
correctly blocked the failure clause. `smin` cannot buy the octaves — measured,
0.05 to 0.001 moved realized octaves by 0.04. The **frac arm (0.60/0.35/0.15 at
smin 0.01) is written and unmeasured**; it tests whether r1 is the distance to a
sibling rather than the parent. One run, ~25 minutes, in
`harness/rc1/r12_stage2b.py`.

**Proposal for round 13 (not tested):** a Mandelbrot *multiplicative cascade*,
not the Mandelbrot set. A self-similar measure is subsample-covariant by
definition, which is exactly the round-11 diagnosis (real hub mass is a
population law that re-expresses at every scale; fixed owners cannot do that).
A multifractal also gives G7's local-dimension spectrum natively and the
concentration the EC post-mortem said was the hard property. Risk: it may
undersample at n=25k in 1024 dims exactly as `ec_torus` did, so pick parameters
with effective support dimension near 52 and check that first.

## 7. Ops facts worth not rediscovering

- `nats-bursting` publishes submit errors to `burst.status.<job_id>` and never
  logs them. Subscribe to `burst.status.>` BEFORE publishing or a rejected
  descriptor looks identical to nothing happening.
- Admission requires an explicit `ephemeral-storage` limit or the job is
  rejected outright.
- `burst.submit` cannot set nodeSelector or tolerations (documented code gap).
  Use a manifest for anything needing placement.
- Only 123 nodes cluster-wide have 6+ CPU and 8Gi allocatable untainted; only
  three sites have 8 or more. UNL has 43 Ready nodes but 41 are tainted.
- Atlas has no thermal headroom for CPU work while Erebus runs. An OMP=2 ovb job
  took package 0 to 92C. Use NRP.
- Editing a running bash script corrupts it — bash re-reads by byte offset.
  Fix the repo copy and restart.
